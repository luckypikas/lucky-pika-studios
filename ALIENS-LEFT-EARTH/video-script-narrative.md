📺 VIDEO SCRIPT: WHY THE ALIENS LEFT EARTH
Estimated Run Time: ~10-12 minutes
Tone: Cynical, gritty, late-night whistleblower, late-2020s noir.

[0:00 - 1:30] SECTION 1: THE DISCONTINUED FILE
(Tone: Cold, steady, leaning into the microphone)
NARRATOR:
Six years ago, during the first wave of the pandemic, if you told people you saw a gray entity in a dining room in East Charlotte, the CMPD locked you in a psych holding cell.
Today? The government releases hundreds of unclassified UFO documents, video of objects shot down over the Middle East, and people just scroll past it on their phones to watch a dance trend. We’re too distracted to care.
The brass told me our 2020 file on the missing Marcus kid and the warehouse worker, Lou Jackson, was accidentally purged during a routine server migration. That’s code for a federal vacuum. If you search the database for Lou’s name, or the bullet we pulled out of that drywall on East Trade Street, you get a "File Not Found" error.
I know because I check every single month.
I was sitting in my living room recently, watching an episode of The Why Files on YouTube. The host was doing a deep dive on black-budget spacecraft and engineered population resets. Suddenly, I felt the hair on my arms stand up. He quoted an old, obscure leak about an impending "Impact Winter"—a climate reset designed to clean out the surface populace.
It was almost word-for-word what that twenty-three-year-old kid, Lou, mumbled into my tape recorder before he vanished. The Why Files thought it was just a compelling conspiracy theory.
But I sat there in the dark knowing it was real. Because I’m the old-school detective who still has the ruined micro-cassette to prove it.

[1:30 - 4:15] SECTION 2: THE JEDI AND THE HUSTLER
(Tone: Narrative, painting the picture of the characters)
NARRATOR:
The case started on Saturday night, October twenty-fourth, 2020. At exactly eleven-fifteen p.m., a couple on Charlotte's East side received a bizarre phone call from a strange number. It was their son, Austin Frye.
Austin was a twenty-three-year-old EMT. A total "square." The hardest thing he ever drank was a Sprite. He was completely obsessed with Star Wars, practiced tai chi, and spent his nights meditating, looking for what he called his "higher purpose."
On the phone, Austin sounded completely cold. He told his folks he was leaving for India immediately to volunteer at a Covid clinic. He couldn't name the town. He wasn't coming home to pack. Before they could ask about his brand-new Toyota Camry, he hung up. The parents tried calling back, but it just spat a busy signal. No voicemail. Nothing.
The CMPD didn't give a damn at first. They thought the kid cracked under pandemic stress and ran off with his secret college girlfriend, Kayla.
But the timeline didn't fit. We pulled the GPS logs from Austin’s new Camry. It put the car miles away from his parents, idling outside the house of a small-time weed dealer named Louis "Trip" Jackson.
Lou was the polar opposite of Austin. A high school dropout who got his GED just to stop his mama from fussing. Six feet tall, slim, always rocking a doo-rag and a flashing chain with his personal brand logo. Lou wanted to be a rap mogul like Rick Ross or Master P. He cussed constantly and treated street thugs and police detectives with the exact same casual disrespect.
When my partner and I pulled Lou into the box on a grand theft auto charge, he tried to play the tough-guy entrepreneur. But when we threatened him with a federal triple-homicide indictment, his entire hustle evaporated. He made us swear on our children’s lives that we weren't working for the CIA, the FBI, or "hybrid-human aliens."
Then he told us about October thirteenth behind the Home Depot on Wendover Road.

[4:15 - 7:00] SECTION 3: THE DIAMOND SALT
(Tone: Shifting into pure, unsettling sci-fi reality)
NARRATOR:
Lou told us he got off a city bus at ten-fifteen p.m. to buy beer, taking a shortcut across the train tracks behind the Home Depot. Suddenly, he heard taser-like popping sounds and saw flashes of light near the homeless camp by the bridge privacy fence.
Then he saw a man leap completely over a six-foot fence in a single bound, toss a flashing device that left people screaming in agony on the other side, and crash into the bushes. Lou dives behind a stack of mulch. The next second, a deafening hum vibrates straight through his bones, a sharp pain spikes his skull, and a low-flying, cloaked craft buzzes the parking lot so hard Lou passes out in the dirt, soaking his hoodie in spilled beer.
When Lou woke up, the jumper was gone. But lying in the grass was a dark metallic cylinder.
Lou and his handy-man buddy Dyon used a commercial bench vice to crack the seals. Inside sat a pristine bed of white and blue crystals that sparkled like crushed diamonds. Lou sniffed it. It was completely odorless. To test it, they literally fed a sample to a local street user named Moot in the back of a parked sedan. Moot tasted it, said it tasted like sweet licorice, and sat there completely sober for an hour. Moot told them it was probably just dummy chemical junk for a pandemic vaccine.
Lou thought he was holding trash. He didn't realize that the crystals were completely dormant because they were missing the final catalyst: human adrenaline.
Austin Frye was the EMT on the call that picked up the burned man from the tracks. Austin figured out what Lou had. He tracked Lou down through the dispatch logs, left a sticky note on his door offering a two-hundred-and-fifty-dollar bribe, and eventually forced a meeting.
By Friday, Lou sold Austin a forty-gram sample in a plastic sandwich bag for two thousand dollars cash. Lou thought he was a king pin. He had no idea Austin was taking that sample to his financier—a fifty-three-year-old retired mail carrier named Doc who lived off Pineville-Matthews Road.

[7:00 - 9:30] SECTION 4: THE RESETS AND THE SHOCK-TROOPS
(Tone: Deeply paranoid, uncovering the cosmic lore)
NARRATOR:
During the interrogation, Lou summarized what Austin told him during a desperate twenty-minute phone call. Austin had already ingested the pure formula after consulting with a black-budget scientist. Lou said Austin was physically changing before his eyes—growing taller, heavily muscled, his face bolder, walking with a terrifying supernatural pep.
Austin told Lou the truth about the formula. It wasn't a narcotic. It was an interstellar stabilizer required to survive an automated eight-year hibernation transit on spacecraft managed entirely by artificial intelligence systems.
Austin dropped the entire cosmic map on him. Seventy years ago, a non-hostile alien faction mapped out a hyper-nutritional target planet called Teo 7 for humanity. The unique planetary vegetation behaves like a biological catalyst—mutating human DNA back to its purest, ancient form, accelerating muscle synthesis and consciousness until humans become supernatural.
But it triggered a galactic civil war. A hostile hybrid faction—the Nephilim—hates Earthlings. They view us as primitive descendants of ancient Reptilians who polluted the planet after the Great Flood. They don't want humans inheriting Teo 7.
And the world governments? They’ve known about the 2030 surface catastrophe for decades. They’re actively building underground cities and fueling exodus ships for the elite—scientists, engineers, and politicians. They view the upcoming surface depopulation as a logistical blessing. They even deploy "gangstalkers" to harass the homeless because they're terrified that unhoused people and off-grid preppers actually have the survival skills to live through the freeze and disrupt the reset.
Austin offered Lou and his family a guaranteed manifest on the ships. But Lou refused. Lou told us: "Austin is crazy, man. He’s gonna end up looking like one of them gray hybrid monsters. I didn't want to turn into no alien. I just wanted to take my money, buy a nice car, and enjoy whatever time I got left on this dirt."
Lou didn't care about the universe. He hung up on a space prophet because he had a date waiting in his driveway.

[9:30 - END] SECTION 5: THE EVACUATION
(Tone: Heavy, dramatic finish, slowing down the pace)
NARRATOR:
The final transaction was set for Sunday afternoon, October twenty-fifth, at Doc’s house. Doc didn't care about human currency—he was a detached, neutral observer. He stuffed a secret one hundred thousand dollars into a black tote bag for Lou just because he saw the kid loved money. Lou stashed seventy grand behind his closet baseboards and told everyone he only got forty.
But before the cash could change hands, the garage door exploded.
Three Nephilim hybrid assassins breached the room. Lou reached for his illegal strap, but a kinetic projectile shattered his bicep, sending his gun flying. While Doc cleared the floor in a single flying kick, Austin went completely berserker with a heavy iron fireplace poker, beating the remaining two hitters to death in a thirty-second bloodbath.
They loaded the three non-human corpses into the back of Doc's twenty-fourteen Chevy Tahoe. Austin, bleeding from a chest wound that was already miraculously regenerating, handed Lou the keys to his Camry for keeps. They executed a vehicle swap on Independence Boulevard and vanished into the night.
We had to let Lou go. The forensic team found the bullet in the drywall, the long brown hair belonging to Kayla, and Dyon's bare fingerprint on the outside doorknob. But with no bodies and pandemic-shortened scheduling, we couldn't hold them. We set up surveillance, waiting for Lou to lead us to Austin.
But the universe broke Lou anyway.
On Thanksgiving, his mama caught the virus. By December twenty-second, she was dead. On Christmas Eve, his little brother’s biological dad stripped the kid of his necessities and drove away, leaving Lou entirely alone in an empty house on Christmas Day. In January, he was laid off from his warehouse job. The police department refused to return his seized cash.
On January eighth, his relatives swarmed the house like vultures, stripping his mama’s remaining belongings. Lou sat in the dark with seventy thousand dollars of hidden alien cash that couldn't buy back a single thing that mattered.
A week later, his cousin unlocked the front door with a spare key. The house was a wreck. Lou was completely gone. His mama’s car was found abandoned on a side street. Lou never made it to his second interrogation.
Most people look at the skies today, watching military leaks and billionaire rocket launches, wondering when the aliens are going to arrive.
They’ve got the whole dynamic backward. The timeline is running out, the automated colony ships are warming their engines in the underground hangars, and the elites have already signed the manifests.
This isn't a story about an invasion. This is the logbook of an evacuation.
And now you finally know the truth... behind why the aliens left Earth.
